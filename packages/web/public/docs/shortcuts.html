Keyboard shortcuts

Your own keys

The keys below are the ones the app ships with. The general shortcuts — the command palette, the sidebar, focus mode, pinning, the shortcut list, the queries, Ask AI, the assistant, moving a tab and split view — can be given other keys in Account → Keyboard shortcuts: choose Change and press the new keys. Keys another shortcut uses, keys the browser or editing keep (copy, paste, closing a tab, reloading…) and a plain letter or sign that would fire while you type are refused, with the reason. Reset gives one shortcut its own keys back (as does pressing those keys again), Reset all every one; nothing can be changed until your keys have been read from the server, and Retry asks again. Keys a newer version can no longer use go back to the shortcut's own, and the settings say which, once. Your keys are kept for you on the server, so your other browsers have them too; the ? list, the command palette, tooltips and screen readers name your keys. The keys of editors, lists, tables, the diagram and the shell stay as they are.

Split view shows two tabs side by side. Open it with Open beside in a tab's menu (its arrow, a right click or Shift + F10), by dragging a tab to the right side of the workspace, or with Ctrl / ⌘ + \. There is one tab strip: the two tabs on screen carry a pane marker, and a tab chosen in the strip opens in the pane in use. The pane in use has the accent bar and says Active pane; click or tab into the other one to use it. Drag the divider, or focus it and use the arrow keys, to share the width (20–80 %). Close pane in a pane's header ends the split and keeps both tabs open, the keyboard's focus in the pane left; closing a pane's tab ends it too. Home hides the split without ending it: choosing one of its tabs, or Ctrl / ⌘ + \ on Home, shows it again (with no split kept, Ctrl / ⌘ + \ on Home puts the two tabs used last side by side). Ctrl / ⌘ + \ moves the keyboard's focus into the pane in use, and moving between the panes adds no step to the browser's history. The split comes back after a reload, as the tabs do. Below 768 px wide only one tab is shown — the split is kept for a wider window — and Open beside simply opens the tab. Screen readers find each pane as a region named after its tab ("orders, left pane").

While you work elsewhere — another database, another page — the collection tabs of a database (two or more, with the database's own tab when it is open) gather into one tab named with the database and how many tabs it holds; pointed at or focused, it names them. Clicking it or Enter on it opens the database itself (its tab, opened if it was not) and shows them again; opening one of its collections anywhere shows them too, that collection active. The arrows stop on it without opening it; Delete, its close button or a middle click closes them all, and Undo brings them back. Ctrl / ⌘ + Shift + arrows move the active tab past a gathered group as one; a group can be dragged.
